## Description

Most people pay $10,000-$30,000 to clear rural land. What if the government paid 90% of that for you?

EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program) is a USDA cost-share program that reimburses landowners for conservation work — brush clearing, fencing, ponds, tree planting. If you qualify as a beginning farmer, you get up to 90% back.

This video walks you through every step: how to get a farm number, hire a consulting forester, apply for EQIP, and stack a timber sale with the cost-share for two income streams from one piece of land.

## Script (Bullet-Point Style)

### COLD OPEN (0:00 - 1:00)
- "What if I told you the government would pay you to clear your land? Not a loan. Not a tax credit. An actual check — up to 90% of the cost — deposited into your account after you do the work."
- "Most people have never heard of this program. I hadn't either until I started researching how to build a homestead without going broke. This one program changed how I look at every piece of land I evaluate."
- "The program is called EQIP. And by the end of this video, you'll know exactly how to use it."

### WHAT IS EQIP (1:00 - 2:30)
- "EQIP stands for Environmental Quality Incentives Program. It's run by the USDA through an agency called NRCS — the Natural Resources Conservation Service."
- "Here's the deal in plain English: you do conservation work on your land — clearing invasive brush, building fences for rotational grazing, digging stock ponds, planting trees — and the government reimburses you a percentage of the cost."
- "The standard rate is 75%. But if you qualify as a beginning farmer — meaning you've been farming for less than 10 years — that goes up to 90%."
- "Let me say that again. On a $20,000 brush clearing job, they send you $18,000. Your out of pocket: $2,000."
- "It's not a loan. You don't pay it back. It's a reimbursement for improving the land."
- Screen share: show the NRCS EQIP program page

### WHO QUALIFIES (2:30 - 3:30)
- "Here's the eligibility checklist. It's simpler than you think."
- Show on screen:
  1. Own or control the land (deed or lease)
  2. Have a farm number from FSA (free, takes an hour)
  3. Land has a resource concern (overgrown land qualifies easily)
  4. Adjusted gross income under $900,000
- "If you just bought raw rural land with brush on it, you almost certainly qualify."
- "And here's the kicker: 5% of all EQIP funds nationally are set aside specifically for beginning farmers. Another 5% for socially disadvantaged producers. These are dedicated pools — you're not competing against the 50-year rancher next door."
- "As a beginning farmer, you can also get 50% of the money UPFRONT before you do the work. That's the advance payment option."

### THE 10-STEP PROCESS (3:30 - 5:30)
- "Here's the exact sequence. I'm going to go through all 10 steps."
- Screen share or graphics showing the steps:
  1. Get your farm number from FSA (Day 1, free, 1 hour)
  2. Visit the NRCS office (usually same building), meet your district conservationist
  3. Hire a consulting forester for a timber cruise
  4. Forester delivers a management plan
  5. NRCS develops a conservation plan with you
  6. Submit EQIP application (Form CPA-1200 — your conservationist helps)
  7. Application enters the ranking period
  8. Contract offered if you rank high enough
  9. Implement practices AFTER the contract is signed
  10. NRCS certifies your work, payment issued
- Walk through each step briefly. Emphasize: "Your district conservationist is your best friend in this process. They're not salespeople. They genuinely want to help you succeed."

### THE TIMBER + EQIP STACK (5:30 - 7:00)
- "Now here's where it gets really good. This is the play that most people don't know about."
- "Step 1: the consulting forester inventories all the timber on your property. Species, size, quality, market value."
- "Step 2: the forester sends that inventory out for bids. Timber companies compete to buy your standing timber."
- "Step 3: the winning company comes in and harvests the trees. They pay YOU. On 25 acres of mixed hardwood in eastern Oklahoma, that could be $5,000 to $30,000 depending on what's there."
- "Now your land has stumps, brush, and slash left over. This is where EQIP comes in."
- "Step 4: EQIP pays you to clear that brush and convert the land to productive pasture or agricultural use."
- "So let's add it up: the timber company pays you $15,000. EQIP reimburses 90% of the $10,000 brush clearing job — that's $9,000. You just made $24,000 and your land went from overgrown forest to clean, productive pasture."
- "Your out of pocket: $1,000 for the brush clearing. That's it."

### THE ONE RULE (7:00 - 7:30)
- "There is one rule you absolutely cannot break."
- Pause. Direct to camera. Serious tone.
- "Do NOT start any work before your EQIP contract is signed. If you clear brush, build a fence, or dig a pond before the contract is signed — that work is NOT eligible for reimbursement. Period. No exceptions. No waivers."
- "I've heard stories of people losing $20,000 because they got excited and started clearing before the paperwork was done. Don't be that person."

### REALISTIC TIMELINE (7:30 - 8:30)
- "Let's be honest about timing. This is not instant."
- Show timeline:
  - Day 1: Close on land, get farm number, visit NRCS
  - Week 1-4: Hire forester, timber cruise, management plan
  - Week 4-8: Submit EQIP application
  - Month 3-6: Application ranked, contract offered
  - After contract: Implement, certify, get paid
- "The national batching deadline is January 15. If you miss it, your application rolls to the next ranking period."
- "Meanwhile, you CAN sell the timber immediately — that's a private transaction, no EQIP involvement. Just don't touch the brush until the contract is signed."

### WHERE TO START (8:30 - 9:30)
- "Here's what you do Monday morning."
- "Step 1: Go to farmers.gov/service-center-locator. Type in your county. That gives you the address and phone number for your local FSA and NRCS office. They're usually in the same building."
- "Step 2: Call and schedule an appointment. Tell them you just bought land and you want to apply for EQIP."
- "Step 3: While you're there, get your farm number from FSA."
- "That's three things. One phone call and one visit. Everything else flows from there."
- Screen share: farmers.gov service center locator
