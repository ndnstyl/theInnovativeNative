## Script (Bullet-Point Style — Mike speaks naturally)

### COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:30)
- "We're buying 25 acres of land. We're building a house on it with our own hands. We're growing food on it. And we're structuring it so our daughters inherit it without a courtroom fight. This is the full plan."
- Quick montage of: land photos, budget spreadsheet, floor plan sketch, family photo (if available)

### SECTION 1: WHY (0:30 - 1:30)
- "Let me explain why we're doing this. It's not because we hate cities. I've lived in cities my whole career."
- Talk about: wanting something that lasts, wanting the girls to have skills and options, wanting to control your food/water/power
- "We're not prepping for doomsday. We're building a foundation that our grandchildren can stand on."
- Emotional anchor: "Every generation should leave the next one stronger than they found it."

### SECTION 2: THE BUDGET (1:30 - 3:15)
- "Here's the number: $275,000 in cash reserves. That's the cap. No debt. No income touched."
- Pull up the budget breakdown (screen share or graphics):
  - Land 25 ac: $57K
  - Well + cistern + septic: $30K
  - Post-frame shell + envelope: $68K
  - Solar + battery: $16K
  - Interior finish: $36K
  - Aquaponics + trees + fence: $20K
  - Tractor + tools: $25K
  - Contingency: $25K
- "The secret is 90% DIY. If I hired contractors for everything, this would be $337K. By doing it ourselves, it's $272K."
- Pro tip: "The biggest thing people get wrong is they don't account for the sequence. You can't build a house before you have water. The order matters."

### SECTION 3: WHY OKLAHOMA (3:15 - 4:45)
- "We looked at Texas and Oklahoma. Oklahoma won."
- Quick comparison:
  - Land cost: OK $2,000-$2,600/ac vs TX $3,000-$5,000
  - Water rights: OK has a domestic exemption — no permit needed. TX has GCDs that can restrict you.
  - Building permits: many eastern OK counties have zero residential code enforcement
  - Ag exemption: no rollback trap like Texas has (TX charges 5 years of back taxes if you change use)
- "Eastern Oklahoma — Haskell, Latimer, Le Flore counties. 48-55 inches of rain. Cheap land. Leave-you-alone government."

### SECTION 4: FINDING LAND (4:45 - 6:30)
- "Here's how you actually find land that works."
- Channels: LandWatch, local agents, county GIS mining, direct mail to absentee owners
- "We sent 500 letters to landowners who've held property for 20+ years and live somewhere else. Response rate: 1-3%. But those deals are 20-30% below market."
- The scorecard: 10 factors, rate each 1-5. If the total is under 35, walk.
- Non-negotiables: clear title, legal access, not in a floodplain, water available, no active mineral lease

### SECTION 5: DUE DILIGENCE (6:30 - 8:00)
- "This is the part that saves you. The sexy part is buying the land. The important part is the homework."
- Three phases: pre-offer, post-offer, pre-close
- Key checks: title search, mineral rights (severed?), FEMA flood map, neighbor well logs, perk test for septic
- "If minerals are severed AND there's active O&G within 5 miles — we walk. Period. A drill pad on your homestead destroys everything."
- "Hire a real estate attorney for $500. Best money you'll spend."
- B-roll idea: screen share of FEMA flood map, county GIS portal

### SECTION 6: EQIP — THE GAME CHANGER (8:00 - 10:00)
- "This is the part nobody talks about. The government will pay you to clear your land."
- Explain EQIP in 90 seconds:
  - What it is: USDA cost-share program. They reimburse 75-90% of conservation work.
  - What it covers: brush clearing, fencing, ponds, tree planting
  - "As a beginning farmer, we qualify for the 90% rate. On a $10,000 clearing job, we pay $1,000."
- The timber play: "First, a consulting forester inventories the trees. Timber companies bid on them — you get paid. Then EQIP pays to clear the brush that's left. Money comes IN at both stages."
- Timeline reality check: "The catch: 3-6 months from application to contract. You cannot start work before the contract is signed or you're disqualified."
- "I built a full guide with every phone number and step at the link in the description."

### SECTION 7: THE BUILD (10:00 - 11:30)
- "We're building a barndominium. Metal post-frame shell, fully finished residential interior."
- Why: 30% cheaper than stick frame, faster to build, incredibly durable
- The envelope: closed-cell spray foam, continuous exterior insulation, mini-split heat pumps, ERV
- "7 things we hire out: well drilling, septic, spray foam, electrical tie-in, grid inspection, truss crew, and the engineer stamp. Everything else — every wall, every pipe, every outlet — is us."
- Solar: 6kW panels + 20kWh battery, grid-tied but can run off-grid
- B-roll ideas: barndominium photos, wall cross-section diagram

### SECTION 8: FOOD SYSTEMS (11:30 - 13:00)
- "Year 2 is when the food systems come online."
- Aquaponics: tilapia + vegetables in a greenhouse. Year-round production. ~$6K to build.
- Trees: 60 bare-root in Year 1 — pecans, walnuts, persimmon, mulberry. Pecans produce Year 5+.
- Livestock: 8-10 hair sheep, a livestock guardian dog, 12 laying hens
- "Our target: 40% food self-sufficiency by Year 2. 50%+ by Year 5."
- "We're not trying to become full-time farmers. The business keeps running. This is about options."

### SECTION 9: THE LEGAL STRUCTURE (13:00 - 14:00)
- "Here's the part most homesteaders skip and regret."
- Buy personally → transfer to revocable living trust → LLC operates the farm
- "The trust means when I die, the land passes to my daughters automatically. No probate court. No $20K in attorney fees. No 18-month wait."
- "The stepped-up basis — this is the biggest tax win. All the appreciation during my lifetime? Never taxed. Ever."
- "This costs $3,000-$5,000 to set up. It saves tens of thousands."

### SECTION 10: CLOSE (14:00 - 15:00)
- "This is a real plan. Not a dream board. Every number is researched, every step is sequenced, every risk has a plan."
- "I built a full resource hub with all of this — checklists, contacts, phone numbers, calculators, the EQIP guide, the legal structure breakdown. It's free, it's password-protected, and it's for people who are actually doing this."
- CTA: "Link in the description. The password is in the pinned comment."
- Close: "Claim the land. Build the village."
