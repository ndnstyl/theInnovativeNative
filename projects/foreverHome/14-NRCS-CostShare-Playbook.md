## What Is EQIP?

**EQIP (Environmental Quality Incentives Program)** is the USDA's largest conservation cost-share program, run through NRCS (Natural Resources Conservation Service). The government reimburses you for doing conservation work on your land — clearing invasive brush, building cross-fencing for rotational grazing, digging stock ponds, planting trees, improving soil, managing water. There are 170+ eligible conservation practices, each with a numbered code.

**The core deal**: You do the work (or hire someone), NRCS reimburses you a percentage of the cost after they inspect and certify that it meets their standards.

### Eligibility Requirements

You must meet ALL of these:

1. **Own or control the land** — deed or lease. Cannot apply speculatively before you own it.
2. **Have a farm number from FSA** — free, obtained at your local FSA office with deed + ID + SSN. This is the prerequisite that unlocks all USDA programs.
3. **Land must have an identified resource concern** — erosion, invasive brush, degraded pasture, poor water quality, etc. Raw or overgrown land qualifies easily.
4. **Comply with conservation provisions** — sign Form AD-1026 (Highly Erodible Land and Wetland Conservation Certification). NRCS helps you fill this out. It ensures you're not farming highly erodible land without a conservation plan and that you're protecting any wetlands on the property.
5. **Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) under $900,000** — averaged over 3 prior tax years. Not an issue for most beginning farmers.
6. **Be in compliance with the Farm Bill** — essentially means no prior USDA fraud.

**No minimum acreage.** No requirement to already be farming. Brand-new landowners qualify. First-time farmers qualify.

### Cost-Share Rates

| Producer Type | Cost-Share Rate | Advance Payment Available? |
|---------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| Standard producer | **75%** of practice cost | No |
| **Beginning farmer** (farmed <10 yrs) | **Up to 90%** | **Yes — 50% upfront before work starts** |
| **Socially disadvantaged** (racial/ethnic minority) | **Up to 90%** | **Yes — 50% upfront** |
| **Veteran farmer** | **Up to 90%** | **Yes — 50% upfront** |
| Limited resource farmer | **Up to 90%** | **Yes — 50% upfront** |

These categories stack with dedicated funding pools: 5% of all EQIP funds nationally are reserved for beginning farmers, and another 5% for socially disadvantaged producers. These are separate pots — you're not competing against the established 50-year rancher next door.

### The Advance Payment Option

For beginning, socially disadvantaged, limited resource, and veteran producers:
- NRCS deposits **at least 50%** of the contracted payment BEFORE you implement the practice
- You use it to purchase materials or hire contractors
- Must be spent within **90 days** of receipt
- Practice must be completed per the EQIP plan of operations
- Remaining balance paid after NRCS certifies the completed work

This is critical for cash-flow — instead of fronting $10,000 and waiting months for reimbursement, you get $5,000 deposited upfront.

### Eligible Practices (Relevant to Your Build)

Each practice has a numbered code. You select which ones to include in your application:

| Code | Practice | What It Pays For |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **314** | Brush Management | Clearing brush, stumps, slash. Forestry mulcher, dozer, chemical, prescribed fire. |
| **382** | Fence | Cross-fencing for rotational grazing ($1.50–$6.00/ft depending on type/state). **Note**: boundary fence replacement ranks very low. Cross-fencing for managed grazing ranks high. |
| **378** | Pond | Stock pond construction — excavation or embankment. Per cubic yard payment. |
| **338** | Prescribed Burning | Controlled burns for invasive brush/grass management. |
| **550** | Range Planting | Establishing native grasses on cleared land. |
| **612** | Tree/Shrub Establishment | Planting trees — your agroforestry plan qualifies. |
| **490** | Forest Stand Improvement | Thinning, releasing crop trees, managing forest health. |
| **516** | Pipeline | Water pipeline to livestock troughs/facilities. |
| **642** | Water Well | Livestock water wells (not domestic). |

### Application & Ranking Process

1. **Get farm number** from FSA (Day 1 after closing, free, 30-60 min appointment)
2. **Visit NRCS office** — meet your district conservationist (usually same building as FSA)
3. **NRCS develops a conservation plan with you** — they walk your property, identify resource concerns, recommend which practices address them
4. **Submit application** (Form CPA-1200) — NRCS staff helps you complete it at the office
5. **Application enters a ranking period** — scored against other applications in your area
6. **Contract offered** if your application ranks high enough — lists approved practices, payment rates per unit, and implementation schedule
7. **You sign the contract** — NOW you can start work (not before)
8. **Implement the practices** — do it yourself or hire contractors
9. **Request NRCS certification** — NRCS staff comes to your property, inspects the work, verifies it meets their Conservation Practice Standards
10. **Payment issued** — reimbursement deposited to your account (typically in October for practices certified that fiscal year)

### How Applications Are Ranked (What Scores Higher)

EQIP is competitive — not everyone gets funded. Scoring criteria:

- **Severity of resource concern** — badly degraded, overgrown, erosion-prone land scores higher than land already in good shape
- **Multiple practices** — applying for brush management + fencing + tree planting + range planting scores better than a single practice
- **Local Working Group priorities** — each state/county sets priority resource concerns. Oklahoma's eastern counties prioritize brush management, pasture improvement, water quality, and wildlife habitat
- **Beginning/underserved status** — gets ranking preference in addition to higher cost-share rates and dedicated funding pools
- **Having a forest management plan** — from a consulting forester. Shows you're serious and have a professional assessment of the land's needs
- **Whole-farm approach** — connecting practices into a coherent conservation system scores better than isolated practices

### NRCS Certification — How It Works

After you complete a practice, NRCS must certify it before you get paid:

1. **You notify NRCS** that the practice is complete
2. **NRCS field staff visits your property** — they measure, inspect, and photograph the completed work
3. **They verify against the Conservation Practice Standard** for that code (e.g., Code 314 has specific standards for brush removal thresholds, treated area percentage, species management, etc.)
4. **Certification or corrective action** — if it meets standards, they certify. If not, they tell you what needs to be corrected before certification.
5. **Payment processes after certification** — typically disbursed in October for the fiscal year

**Key**: NRCS standards are specific. For brush management, they'll verify the target brush species were actually removed to the specified threshold. For fencing, they'll check post spacing, wire gauge, brace construction, gate placement. For ponds, they'll verify capacity, spillway, and embankment specs. Your district conservationist will give you the exact standards BEFORE you start work so you know what to build to.

### What EQIP Does NOT Cover

- Your house, barndominium, or dwelling construction
- Driveways or roads for personal access (ag access roads can qualify)
- Cosmetic landscaping
- **Any work done BEFORE the EQIP contract is signed** — this is the #1 disqualifier
- Boundary fence replacement (ranks very low — cross-fencing for grazing ranks high)
- Equipment purchases (it pays for the *practice*, not the tools you use)
- Maintenance on existing practices within their lifespan (e.g., re-sedimenting an existing pond <20 years old)

### Batching / Ranking Deadlines

- **National batching deadline**: January 15 annually for the first funding round
- Applications are accepted **year-round** but ranked in batches
- Each state may have **additional local ranking periods** — Oklahoma typically has 2-3 per fiscal year
- If you miss one batching deadline, your application automatically rolls to the next period
- **Ask your district conservationist** for the next local ranking date — this is the single most important question to ask at your first NRCS visit

### Timber Sale vs EQIP — Two Separate Money Streams

These are completely independent:

**Timber sale** = private transaction between you and a timber company. Consulting forester inventories your trees → timber companies bid → winner harvests → they pay YOU. No government involvement. Happens on your timeline. Revenue is yours.

**EQIP** = government cost-share for what happens AFTER the timber is removed. The timber company takes the trees. You're left with brush, stumps, slash, and bare ground. EQIP pays to clear that and convert the land to productive agriculture. Requires application, ranking, contract, implementation, and NRCS certification.

You can stack both: timber company pays you to remove the trees, then NRCS pays you to clear what's left. The land goes from overgrown forest to productive pasture, and money flows IN at both stages.

## The Opportunity — Fact-Checked

A series of Facebook reels (Andrew Jordan / AJ Farms) described a process for getting PAID to clear rural land through USDA programs. Here's what's real, what's oversimplified, and what applies to you.

### What the Reels Claimed vs Reality

| Claim | Verdict | Reality |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Get a farm number from FSA | **TRUE** | Free, 30-60 min appointment. Required before anything else. |
| Hire a consulting forester for timber cruise | **TRUE** | Standard practice. They inventory timber, assign market value, solicit bids. |
| NRCS Brush Management (Code 314) pays to clear brush | **TRUE with caveats** | EQIP cost-shares 75% of brush clearing cost (up to 90% for beginning/underserved producers). But it's REIMBURSEMENT, not a check upfront. |
| NRCS covers "up to 90%" of fencing | **PARTIALLY TRUE** | 75% standard rate. 90% ONLY for beginning farmers, socially disadvantaged, limited resource, or veteran producers. Cross-fencing for rotational grazing ranks well; **boundary fence replacement generally does NOT rank well**. |
| Programs for pond installation | **TRUE** | Practice Code 378 (Pond). Same cost-share percentages. |
| Timber company pays YOU for the trees | **TRUE** | Consulting forester solicits bids. You pocket the timber sale revenue. |

### Critical Gotchas the Reels Left Out

1. **YOU CANNOT START WORK BEFORE YOUR EQIP CONTRACT IS SIGNED.** If you clear brush, build fence, or dig a pond before the contract is approved, that practice is **ineligible for reimbursement**. Period. No exceptions without a pre-approved waiver.
2. **Timeline is 3-6 months from application to contract**, not immediate. The January 15 national batching deadline means applications submitted after that wait for the next ranking period.
3. **Payment is reimbursement AFTER the practice is certified** by NRCS as meeting their standards. You front the money, they pay you back.
4. **Advance payment option exists**: Beginning farmers can get 50% upfront before implementation, but must spend it within 90 days and complete the practice per plan.
5. **You must own or control the land** (deed or lease) before applying. Can't apply speculatively.
6. **A conservation plan is required** — NRCS develops this WITH you, but it takes time.
7. **Not guaranteed funding** — applications are ranked competitively. Higher-priority resource concerns score better.

## Realistic 90-Day Timeline — Mapped to Your ForeverHome Plan

Here's the honest truth about your 90-day goal:

| Day | Activity | EQIP Status |
|-----|----------|-------------|
| **Day 1-30** | Find land, walk parcels, make offers | No EQIP activity possible (need land first) |
| **Day 30-45** | Under contract, due diligence | Still no EQIP (don't own it yet) |
| **Day 45-60** | Close on land | **Day of close**: Get farm number + visit NRCS |
| **Day 46-60** | Hire consulting forester, schedule timber cruise | Forester on property within 1-2 weeks |
| **Day 60-75** | Timber cruise complete, management plan drafted | Submit EQIP application |
| **Day 75-90** | Timber bids received, timber company harvesting | EQIP application in ranking queue |
| **Day 90-180** | Timber cleared, brush remains, waiting on EQIP | **Waiting for contract award (3-6 months)** |
| **Day 180-270** | EQIP contract signed, begin brush clearing | Implementation + reimbursement |

**The bottleneck**: You CAN sell timber and pocket that money immediately. You CANNOT start EQIP-reimbursable brush clearing, fencing, or pond work until the contract is signed (3-6 months after application). Starting before the contract = disqualification.

### How to Optimize the 90-Day Window

1. **Timber sale CAN happen immediately** — no EQIP involvement needed. The forester + timber company is a private transaction. Cash in pocket.
2. **Driveway + homesite clearing CAN happen immediately** — if you're NOT applying for EQIP reimbursement on that specific area. Clear what you need for the build pad, RV pad, and driveway using your existing $6K site prep budget. Just don't submit those areas in your EQIP application.
3. **EQIP brush management applies to the REST of the property** — the 20+ acres you're NOT building on. This is where the cost-share kicks in for converting brush to pasture/ag land.
4. **Fence work**: Build only what you need for immediate livestock/security out of pocket. Apply for EQIP on the cross-fencing for rotational grazing (which ranks highest anyway).
5. **Pond**: Long-term play. Apply in your first EQIP application, implement in Y2-Y3.

### Y0 Q4 → Y1 Q1 — EQIP Contract Period:

- [ ] EQIP contract awarded (3-6 months after application)
- [ ] Request advance payment (50% upfront)
- [ ] Hire forestry mulcher for brush clearing on non-homesite areas
- [ ] Install cross-fencing for rotational grazing (EQIP-reimbursable)
- [ ] NRCS inspects completed practices
- [ ] Final reimbursement received (typically October)

---

## Hard Rules

1. **DO NOT start any EQIP practice before the contract is signed.** This is the #1 mistake. Starting early = disqualified from reimbursement. No exceptions.
2. **Timber sale is separate from EQIP.** You can sell timber anytime — it's your property, your trees, your revenue. No government involvement needed.
3. **Homesite clearing is NOT EQIP.** Clear your building pad, driveway, and RV pad with your own budget ($6K site prep line). EQIP applies to the ag/conservation portions of the property.
4. **Get the farm number FIRST.** Nothing else works without it. Day-of-close errand.
5. **Beginning farmer designation is your leverage.** It unlocks 90% cost-share + advance payments + dedicated funding pools. Don't skip this.
6. **The consulting forester is the gatekeeper.** Without their management plan, NRCS won't engage. Hire them within Week 1 of ownership.
7. **Applications are ranked, not guaranteed.** Submit a strong application. Brush management + rotational grazing + tree establishment for a beginning farmer on degraded land should score well.
8. **Keep every receipt.** EQIP reimbursement requires documentation of actual costs incurred.
