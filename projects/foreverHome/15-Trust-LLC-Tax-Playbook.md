# Trust, LLC & Tax Structure Playbook — Buy Personal, Wrap in Trust, Build Generational

**Created**: 2026-04-20
**Status**: Research complete — needs attorney + CPA validation before execution
**Goal**: Structure the land purchase and homestead to minimize taxes, avoid probate, protect assets, preserve equity borrowing ability, and ensure clean generational transfer to daughters.

---

## The Strategy — Plain English

**Buy the land in your personal name** (simplest financing, cleanest title, full homestead exemption). Then **transfer it into a revocable living trust** that you control during your lifetime. The trust avoids probate, names your daughters as beneficiaries, and keeps your name off public records. Your LLC operates the agricultural business ON the land but doesn't own the land itself.

```
PHASE 1: Buy personally → full homestead + ag exemptions → simplest financing
    │
PHASE 2: Transfer to Revocable Living Trust → avoid probate, you stay trustee
    │
PHASE 3: LLC operates the farm/ranch business ON the trust-held land
    │
PHASE 4: Build equity → borrow against it when needed (trust allows this)
    │
PHASE 5: On your passing → trust transfers to daughters automatically, no court
```

---

## Why This Order Matters

### Why buy personally FIRST (not through LLC or trust)

1. **Financing**: USDA loans, conventional mortgages, and owner financing are all easier as an individual. Lenders don't want to deal with LLC/trust paperwork at closing.
2. **Homestead exemption**: Only available to natural persons in Oklahoma. An LLC cannot claim homestead exemption.
3. **Agricultural exemption**: Easier to establish as an individual with a farm number.
4. **Title insurance**: Simpler and cheaper for individuals.
5. **Seller comfort**: Rural OK sellers prefer dealing with a person, not an entity.

### Why transfer to a revocable living trust AFTER closing

1. **Avoids probate**: When you die, the property passes to your daughters immediately through the trust — no court, no attorney fees, no 6-18 month wait.
2. **You stay in control**: As trustee, you manage everything exactly as you do now. No loss of control whatsoever.
3. **Privacy**: Trust ownership keeps your name off some public records.
4. **No tax event**: Transferring to your own revocable trust is NOT a taxable event. No capital gains, no gift tax.
5. **Ag exemption preserved**: Oklahoma agricultural exemption continues when property is held in a revocable trust.
6. **Homestead exemption preserved**: Oklahoma allows homestead exemption for property in a living/revocable trust.

### Why the LLC operates the business, not owns the land

1. **Liability protection**: If someone gets hurt on the farm, or a business deal goes bad, the LLC shields the land (held in trust) from lawsuits against the business.
2. **Tax flexibility**: LLC can elect S-corp taxation to reduce self-employment tax on farm income above reasonable salary.
3. **Ag sales tax exemption**: Oklahoma LLC engaged in farming/ranching qualifies for agricultural exemption on equipment and supply purchases.
4. **Clean separation**: Land = generational asset (trust). Business = operational entity (LLC). Different purposes, different structures.

## Oklahoma-Specific Legal Facts

### Documentary Stamp Tax on Transfer

**Good news**: Oklahoma exempts transfers from documentary stamp tax when the only owners of the LLC (or trust) are the transferor, their spouse, parent, child, or person related within second degree of consanguinity. ([68 O.S. § 3202](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-68/section-68-3202/))

**Gotcha**: If you transfer any interest in the entity to someone outside that family circle within 1 year, you owe the full stamp tax retroactively.

**For your situation**: Transfer from Mike (personal) → Mike's Revocable Living Trust = **exempt** from documentary stamp tax. You are the grantor, trustee, and beneficiary.

### Property Tax Cap (3% / 5% Rule)

Oklahoma caps annual property tax increases at:
- **3%** for homestead-exempt or agricultural land
- **5%** for all other locally assessed real property

**Critical**: The cap is **lifted when title is transferred** to another person. The property gets reassessed at full fair cash value.

**For your situation**: Transfer to your own revocable trust should NOT trigger reassessment because you remain the beneficial owner. **However, this is not explicitly confirmed in statute** — verify with your county assessor before transferring. If it DOES trigger reassessment, do the transfer immediately after purchase (before the property appreciates).

### Homestead Exemption in Trust

Oklahoma allows the homestead exemption for property held in a living trust or revocable trust, as long as the grantor/beneficiary resides in the home. Your $1,000 homestead exemption continues.

### Agricultural Exemption in Trust

The ag exemption (OTC 994) should continue when property is in a revocable trust, as the trust is a "pass-through" for tax purposes. File the exemption in the trust's name (or your name as trustee). **Verify with county assessor.**

## Texas-Specific Legal Facts

### No Documentary Stamp Tax

Texas does not impose a documentary stamp tax on property transfers. Transfer from personal → trust or personal → LLC has no transfer tax in Texas. This makes restructuring simpler than Oklahoma.

### Property Tax — Ag Exemption (1-d-1)

Texas agricultural exemption works differently than Oklahoma:
- Requires **5 years of continuous agricultural use** before the exemption kicks in
- Buy land that ALREADY has the ag exemption to avoid the 5-year wait
- **Rollback tax**: If you convert ag-exempt land to non-agricultural use, you owe **5 years of back taxes** plus interest at the difference between ag-assessed and market-assessed values. This can be $5K–$50K+ depending on property value. Non-negotiable pain.
- Ag exemption IS preserved when property transfers to a revocable trust — the county appraisal district needs notification

### Homestead Exemption in Trust

Texas offers one of the most generous homestead exemptions in the country:
- **Up to 200 acres** for rural property (vs Oklahoma's 1 acre)
- **$100,000** exemption off school district taxes
- Property in a revocable living trust retains the homestead exemption as long as the grantor occupies it
- Texas homestead protections also shield the property from most creditor judgments

### Texas LLC Formation

- Filing fee: $300 (Articles of Organization with TX Secretary of State) — higher than OK's $100
- No state income tax in Texas — LLC income is only taxed federally
- Texas franchise tax: LLCs with revenue under $2.47M owe no franchise tax (your farm won't hit this)
- Texas community property rules apply — if married, both spouses have rights to LLC interests. Address this in the operating agreement.

### Mineral Rights in Trust

Both states: mineral rights should be explicitly addressed in the trust document.
- If you own unified mineral + surface rights, the trust should hold BOTH
- If minerals are severed (common in both OK and TX), the trust holds the surface rights and any mineral interests you acquire separately
- Have the attorney draft specific mineral rights provisions — generic trust templates almost always miss this

## The Revocable Living Trust — What It Is

### Structure

| Role | Person |
|------|--------|
| **Grantor** (creator) | Mike |
| **Trustee** (manager during your lifetime) | Mike |
| **Successor Trustee** (takes over if you're incapacitated or pass) | Wife, then eldest daughter, then named backup |
| **Beneficiaries** | Daughters (equal shares, or as you specify) |

### What it does

- Holds title to the land and home
- You manage everything as trustee — nothing changes day-to-day
- If you become incapacitated, your successor trustee manages without court intervention (no conservatorship needed)
- When you pass, successor trustee distributes per your instructions — no probate court
- Can include conditions: "daughters cannot sell the land until age 30" or "land stays in the family for 20 years minimum"

### What it does NOT do

- Does NOT protect assets from creditors during your lifetime (revocable trusts are transparent to creditors)
- Does NOT reduce income taxes (pass-through, same as personal)
- Does NOT reduce estate taxes (included in your estate — but federal estate tax exemption is $13.6M+ in 2026, so this is irrelevant for your estate size)
- Does NOT protect from Medicaid look-back (revocable trusts are countable assets)

### Cost to set up

- Attorney: $1,500–$3,500 for a properly drafted revocable living trust with pour-over will, power of attorney, healthcare directive, and deed transfer
- Recording the new deed: $25–$50 at the county clerk
- Annual maintenance: $0 (no filings, no annual reports for a revocable trust)

## The LLC — What It Does

### Structure

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Entity type** | Oklahoma LLC (single-member or multi-member) |
| **Owner/member** | Mike (or the trust, after trust is established) |
| **Purpose** | Operate the agricultural business — livestock sales, timber revenue, market garden, agritourism |
| **Tax election** | Default pass-through initially. Consider S-corp election when farm income exceeds ~$40K/yr to reduce self-employment tax. |

### What the LLC does

- Receives all farm income (livestock sales, timber, produce, EQIP reimbursements)
- Pays all farm expenses (feed, equipment, fuel, supplies)
- Carries liability insurance
- Files Schedule C (or 1065 if multi-member) on your tax return
- Qualifies for Oklahoma agricultural sales tax exemption on equipment/supply purchases
- Provides liability shield — lawsuit against the farm business cannot reach the land in the trust

### What the LLC does NOT do

- Does NOT own the land (trust owns it)
- Does NOT claim homestead exemption (only persons can)
- Does NOT get USDA residential loans (USDA requires natural person for home loans)

### LLC-Trust Relationship

The LLC leases the land from the trust (even if it's a nominal lease). This creates a clean legal boundary:

```
Trust (owns the land) ←── lease agreement ──→ LLC (operates the business)
         │                                           │
    Holds title                               Receives farm income
    Homestead exempt                          Pays farm expenses  
    Ag exempt                                 Carries liability insurance
    Passes to daughters                       Can be dissolved/restructured
```

### Cost to set up

- Oklahoma LLC filing: $100 (Articles of Organization with Secretary of State)
- Registered agent: $0 if you use yourself (or ~$50/yr for a service)
- Annual report: $25
- Operating agreement: $500–$1,000 (attorney-drafted, or DIY for single-member)
- EIN from IRS: free

## Borrowing Against Equity

### With a revocable trust

**You can borrow against trust-held property.** As the trustee, you have full authority to take out a mortgage, home equity loan, or line of credit on trust property. Most lenders treat revocable trust property the same as personally-held property for lending purposes.

Some lenders may ask you to temporarily transfer the property out of the trust for the loan closing, then transfer it back. This is routine and creates no tax event.

### With an irrevocable trust

If you later convert to an irrevocable trust (for Medicaid planning or asset protection), borrowing becomes harder:
- Only the successor trustee can borrow on behalf of the trust
- Fewer lenders will do it
- Higher rates and fees
- Trust document must explicitly allow borrowing

**Recommendation**: Keep the trust revocable during your active years. Only consider converting to irrevocable if/when Medicaid planning becomes relevant (typically age 60+).

### Building equity strategy

1. **Year 0**: Buy at $57K–$225K (depending on raw land vs improved property)
2. **Year 1-2**: Build the barndominium — immediately adds $100K+ in improvement value
3. **Year 3-5**: Ag improvements (fencing, pond, established pasture, orchard) add assessed value
4. **Year 5+**: Property likely worth $350K–$500K+ with improvements
5. **Borrow against**: Home equity line of credit at 70-80% LTV = $245K–$400K available
6. **Use for**: Business expansion, education for daughters, second property, emergency reserve

## Tax Minimization Strategy

### Income tax

| Strategy | How | Savings |
|----------|-----|---------|
| **Ag exemption** | File OTC 994, maintain ag use | 40-60% reduction in assessed property value |
| **Farm expense deductions** | All ag expenses through LLC reduce taxable income | Dollar-for-dollar deduction |
| **Depreciation** | Barn, fencing, equipment, tractor all depreciate on Schedule F | $10K-$20K/yr in early years |
| **Section 179** | Deduct full cost of equipment in year purchased (up to $1.22M) | Immediate deduction vs multi-year |
| **S-corp election** | When farm income exceeds ~$40K, elect S-corp to split salary vs distribution | Save 15.3% SE tax on distribution portion |
| **Timber income** | Long-term capital gains treatment on timber held 1+ year (15% vs ordinary rate) | Significant on $10K-$30K timber sales |
| **Cost-share exclusion** | EQIP payments may be excludable from income under IRC §126 | Potentially exclude 75-90% of EQIP reimbursement |

### Property tax

| Strategy | How |
|----------|-----|
| **Homestead exemption** | File at closing, $1,000 off assessed value (small but automatic) |
| **Agricultural exemption** | File OTC 994, prove ag use → assessed at ag value not market value |
| **3% cap** | Ag land + homestead capped at 3% annual increase |
| **Protest assessments** | If reassessed above market, protest at county level (free) |

### Estate/inheritance tax

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Federal estate tax** | Exemption is $13.6M+ (2026). Your estate is well under. **$0 federal estate tax.** |
| **Oklahoma estate tax** | **None.** Oklahoma has no state estate tax. |
| **Oklahoma inheritance tax** | **None.** Oklahoma has no state inheritance tax. |
| **Stepped-up basis** | When daughters inherit through the trust, their cost basis "steps up" to fair market value at your death. All unrealized capital gains **disappear.** This is massive — if the property is worth $400K at death and you paid $57K, the $343K gain is never taxed. |

**The stepped-up basis is the single biggest tax advantage of the trust-and-hold strategy.** It's why you hold, don't sell. Every dollar of appreciation gets a free pass at death.

- [ ] Draft pour-over will (catches anything not in the trust)
- [ ] Draft durable power of attorney
- [ ] Draft healthcare directive / living will

- [ ] Record new deed transferring property from Mike → Mike as Trustee of [Trust Name]
- [ ] Notify county assessor of trust transfer (confirm homestead + ag exemptions continue)
- [ ] Update property insurance to name trust as insured

### Within 6 Months
- [ ] Form Oklahoma LLC for farm operations
- [ ] Get EIN from IRS
- [ ] Draft LLC operating agreement
- [ ] Create lease agreement: Trust (landlord) → LLC (tenant) for nominal rent
- [ ] Apply for Oklahoma agricultural sales tax exemption for LLC
- [ ] Set up LLC bank account for all farm income/expenses
- [ ] Hire CPA for first-year tax planning (see directory below)

### Annually
- [ ] File LLC tax return (Schedule C or 1065)
- [ ] File personal tax return with farm income/expenses
- [ ] Review ag exemption documentation
- [ ] Review trust — update beneficiaries or terms if family situation changes
- [ ] Review S-corp election trigger (farm income >$40K?)

## What NOT to Do

1. **Don't buy through the LLC.** You lose homestead exemption, USDA loan eligibility, and create financing headaches.
2. **Don't skip the trust.** Without it, your daughters go through probate court — $5K–$20K in fees, 6-18 months of waiting, and public record of all assets.
3. **Don't use an irrevocable trust too early.** You lose control and borrowing flexibility. Revocable is correct until Medicaid planning becomes relevant.
4. **Don't transfer to a trust if you have a USDA loan without checking first.** The Garn-St. Germain Act protects against due-on-sale acceleration for revocable trust transfers — but verify with your lender.
5. **Don't DIY the trust document.** Online templates miss state-specific provisions (mineral rights handling, ag exemption continuity, stepped-up basis optimization). Pay the $2,000–$3,500 for an attorney who knows OK or TX ag land.
6. **Don't forget to re-title everything.** The trust only works if the property is actually deeded to the trust. A trust without the deed transfer is an empty box.
