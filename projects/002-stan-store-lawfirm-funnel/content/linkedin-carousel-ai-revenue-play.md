# LinkedIn Carousel - "AI for law firms is a revenue play, not an IT project"

> Produced by `/goal` (domain: Content Creation). Brand rules applied: no em dashes, cyan primary palette, LinkedIn viral mechanics (short hook, confession arc, urgent close), car-analogy voice, visual density over text density. Format: 8 square (1:1) slides for LinkedIn. STATUS: drafted, NOT posted (outward_send gate).

## Design direction (every slide)
- Palette: cyan primary on near-black. Magenta used once, on slide 6 number only. White body text.
- One idea per slide. Big number or 6-word line, not paragraphs.
- Slide footer: small wordmark, bottom-left. Page dots bottom-right.
- Highlight word per slide rendered cyan at 1.18x scale.

---

### Slide 1 - Hook
**Your firm bought AI. Then parked it.**
_(sub, small:)_ A $40k engine idling in the garage.

### Slide 2 - Confession
I watched a firm drop forty grand on AI and bill nothing new from it.
They treated a revenue engine like a new office printer. Plug it in, admire it, forget it.

### Slide 3 - The reframe
IT projects exist to cut cost.
Revenue plays exist to win clients.
AI for a law firm is the second one. Stop filing it under the first.

### Slide 4 - The car analogy
A turbo is not an upgrade to the stereo.
It is more laps and more wins.
AI that "organizes documents" is a stereo. AI that books a consult at 2am is a turbo.

### Slide 5 - Where the revenue actually hides
Speed to lead. The first firm to call back wins the case.
Dead lead reactivation. You already paid to generate them.
Intake that never sleeps. Nights and weekends are when people get arrested.

### Slide 6 - The math (do it on a napkin)
100 after-hours leads a month.
**30%** never get called back.
Recover half of those and that is 15 signed consults you already paid for.
That is not an IT line item. That is a rainmaker.

### Slide 7 - The tell
If your AI rollout reports to IT, it dies in a ticket backlog.
If it reports to revenue, it gets a number to hit.
Org chart is destiny.

### Slide 8 - CTA (urgent, not a generic question)
Stop measuring AI in tickets closed.
Start measuring it in consults booked.
Which one is your firm doing this week. Reply "revenue" and I will send the 1-page intake-speed audit.

## Caption (post copy)
Most law firms file AI under "IT." That is why it never makes them a dime.

An IT project asks "is it installed." A revenue play asks "how many consults did it book this week."

The firms winning with AI are not the ones with the cleanest document folders. They are the ones whose phone gets answered at 2am, whose dead leads get a second call, whose intake never clocks out.

Park the engine in the garage and it is a cost. Put it on the track and it is a rainmaker.

Slide 8 has the move. Reply "revenue" for the 1-page intake-speed audit.
