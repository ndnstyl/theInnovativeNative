# Voice Clone Recording SOP — The Innovative Native

One-time setup: record 2 minutes of clean audio in your own voice, upload to ElevenLabs, get a voice ID, pin it in `infrastructure/elevenlabs/SKILL.md`. Every future reel voiceover is synthesized from this clone.

**Why the sample matters more than people think:** ElevenLabs locks into whatever energy is in your sample. If you read flat, every reel sounds flat. If you rush, every reel rushes. If you sound tired at 11pm, every reel sounds tired. **Whatever you put in, you get out — permanently.** Budget 30 minutes so you can do it right.

---

## Part 1 — Recording setup (10 min)

### Environment

- **Room:** Small, carpeted if possible. Closet with clothes works great. Avoid kitchens, bathrooms, bare-walled rooms (reverb).
- **Noise:** Kill every fan, AC, fridge, computer fan you can hear. Phone on airplane mode. Close windows.
- **Time of day:** Morning after coffee, or any time your voice feels warm and alert. Not first thing out of bed (raspy), not last thing at night (tired).

### Gear

Minimum viable: iPhone Voice Memos, 6–8 inches from your mouth, held steady.

Better: any USB mic (Shure MV7, Blue Yeti, Rode NT-USB) 6 inches away, pop filter or sock over it.

**Whatever mic you use, use it for the entire recording — don't switch halfway.** The clone locks into your mic's character.

### File spec

- Format: **WAV 44.1kHz mono** if possible, or **320kbps MP3 mono**
- **No** noise reduction, EQ, compression, or normalization — ElevenLabs wants raw audio. Their pipeline handles cleanup. Your processing hurts.
- **No** music bed, no ambient background, no anything but your voice

### Delivery rules (the important ones)

1. **Pace yourself.** ~150 words per minute is natural. If you're hitting 180+, you're rushing — slow down, the clone will sound panicked.
2. **Use the pauses.** Every `—` and `…` in the scripts below is an intentional pause. If you blow past them, the clone can't pause either.
3. **Lean into emphasis naturally.** Don't force it. If a word feels like it should hit, let it hit. If it doesn't, move on.
4. **Don't smile on the authority scripts, do smile on the warm one.** Face position changes voice subtly — the clone picks this up.
5. **One take per script is ideal.** Stops and restarts mid-script confuse the clone. If you flub a line, keep going or restart that script from the top.
6. **Between scripts, pause for 3 seconds** so you can trim cleanly later if needed. Don't stack them back-to-back.
7. **One continuous file, not six separate files.** ElevenLabs Instant Voice Clone averages prosody across all uploaded audio — six files means six different room tones / mic distances / energy levels to average, which muddies the clone. One file lets your voice warm up naturally (your tone peaks around the 60–90s mark) and guarantees consistent environment across all scripts. If you flub a script mid-recording, just pause, breathe, restart that script from the top, and keep rolling — don't create a new file.

## Part 2 — The scripts (read in order, ~2 min total)

Each script is tagged with the energy register it's capturing. Read the tag, take a beat to find that energy, then read the script.

---

### Script 1 — Authority hook energy `[SHARP, CONFIDENT, NO SMILE]`

*Target: 22 seconds*

> Every entrepreneur I know is trying to scale. Almost none of them can tell you the difference between scaling — and stretching. If your systems break the moment you double your output, you weren't built to scale. You were stretched thin… and pretending.
>
> Real scale is quiet. It doesn't feel like hustle. It feels like math.

**Delivery notes:** Open strong. "Scaling — and stretching" has a hard beat between the words, like a setup. "You were stretched thin… and pretending" drops the mic. Last line is almost whispered by comparison. Confident, not aggressive.

---

### Script 2 — Teach + reveal energy `[MEASURED, BUILDING]`

*Target: 20 seconds*

> Here's what nobody tells you about AI automation. The bottleneck is never the tools. It's never the prompt. It's the thinking that happens *before* you touch the keyboard.
>
> If you can't describe the outcome in one sentence — no model will save you. Clarity compounds. Confusion is expensive.

**Delivery notes:** Start with a gentle open ("Here's what nobody tells you"), then tighten as you build. "Clarity compounds. Confusion is expensive." is a two-punch landing — equal weight on both, separated by a hard beat. This is the pattern most of your reel closers will use, so nail this one.

---

### Script 3 — Numbers + brand name `[DELIBERATE, TEACHING]`

*Target: 20 seconds*

> In twenty twenty-six, the average creator spends about seven hours a day on content. Ninety-five percent of it goes unseen.
>
> I built theInnovativeNative dot com to change those numbers. Not with louder marketing — with smarter systems. One idea. Ten distributions. Zero burnout.

**Delivery notes:** Numbers need clear articulation: "twenty twenty-six", "ninety-five percent". Don't rush them. When you say "theInnovativeNative dot com," say it as one word — "the-innovative-native-dot-com" — because that's how you want the clone to say your brand forever. Get this right or re-record this script specifically. The last three phrases ("One idea. Ten distributions. Zero burnout.") are three hard beats — slight pause after each period.

---

### Script 4 — Conversational warmth `[WARM, SLIGHT SMILE, SLOWER]`

*Target: 22 seconds*

> I used to think freedom meant walking away from work. Turns out — freedom is work you chose. Built the way you wanted. Delivered at the pace you can sustain.
>
> That's not a dream. That's a design problem. And design problems — those, you can actually solve.

**Delivery notes:** Slow down about 15%. Let your face relax into a slight smile — you can hear it in the audio. This script teaches the clone how to sound warm without losing authority. "Those, you can actually solve" is the close — conversational, not dramatic. Like you're talking to one person at a coffee table.

---

### Script 5 — Three-truth punchy energy `[SHARP, STACCATO, HOOK MODE]`

*Target: 18 seconds*

> Three truths. One — your audience isn't bored. They're overwhelmed. Two — consistency beats intensity, every time. Three — your story should survive a ninety percent cut. If it can't, it wasn't a story. It was filler.
>
> Cut harder. Ship sharper.

**Delivery notes:** This is your 15-second reel energy. Tight, punchy, almost aggressive pace — but not rushed. The enumeration ("One — Two — Three —") gives the clone a rhythmic template it can reuse for listicle reels. "Cut harder. Ship sharper." is a hard stop.

---

### Script 6 — Sign-off CTA variations `[CONFIDENT, INVITING]`

*Target: 18 seconds — read all three, short pause between*

> If that resonated — you know what to do. Save this. Share it. Tag the friend who needs it.
>
> More at theInnovativeNative dot com.
>
> Follow for the systems that built this. Comment "blueprint" and I'll send it straight to you.

**Delivery notes:** Three different CTAs back to back so the clone learns CTA cadence. Each one is a complete thought — pause cleanly between them. The phrase "theInnovativeNative dot com" appears again here deliberately — every reel will end with some version of this, so the clone needs multiple references to lock it in.

## Part 3 — After recording (5 min)

1. **Listen back** to the full take. If any script sounds phoned in, re-record just that script.
2. **Trim leading and trailing silence** (but leave the between-script 3-sec gaps — they don't hurt).
3. **Export as a single file** — don't split into 6 files. ElevenLabs wants one continuous sample.
4. **Name it:** `mike-innovative-native-clone-sample-2026-04-05.wav` (or .mp3)
5. **Save to:** `remotion-videos/public/vo/_voice-samples/` (create if missing)

## Part 5 — Test before you trust it

Before committing the clone to production reels:

1. Generate a 30-second test VO from Script 1 text using the new voice ID
2. Listen back — does it sound like you or a bad impression of you?
3. If it sounds off: check sample quality (background noise? processing artifacts? rushed pace?) and re-record. Do NOT try to fix a bad clone with settings tweaks — fix the sample.

Good clones nail it on the first upload 90% of the time. If your clone sounds flat, rushed, or tired, **your sample was flat, rushed, or tired.** The clone is an honest mirror.

---

## Part 6 — When to re-clone

**Never**, unless:
- You fundamentally change your brand voice (major rebrand)
- You recorded in a noisy environment and regret it
- You want a second voice for a different brand (keep the old one active)

Re-cloning changes the sound subtly and breaks series consistency. If you've been shipping reels with Clone v1 and re-clone, old reels will sound noticeably different from new ones. Commit to v1.
